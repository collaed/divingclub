📜 The Waters Here — by EauMer

A Homeric odyssey into the depths
🏛️⚓🫧🌊🔱📜

Like Ulysses launching himself toward the unknown across the hostile waters of the Mediterranean, our modern heroes plunge into the frozen bowels of the Fort's Ice Cave. Here, no Cyclops lurks, no Sirens weave their spellbinding songs — only a mineral silence, heavy and absolute, broken solely by the crackling whisper of rising bubbles.

At 17 metres below, where light falters and finally surrenders, rests a forgotten wreck. An aircraft buried nose-first in the silt like a dark cross, arms outstretched toward skies it will never see again. Much like Ulysses descending into Hades to walk among the shades, our divers glide toward this strange submerged altar. Their torches pierce the dense darkness, revealing a hull draped in the slow garments of time. They seek, they brush against, they wonder — and they rise back up, transformed.

But every odyssey demands its trials of strength and discipline. Ionas the Greek and Aurel the Romanian, under the guidance of our captains Pietro and Keran, clad in their protective fleece — their drysuits defying the biting cold like the hide of a golden ram — face a different mountain: ascending from 40 metres of depth. Forty metres of dark, pressing silence, where pressure squeezes the lungs and slows the mind. They train there not out of reckless pride, but out of duty. For in Ulysses' crew, no one lets a companion sink alone. To master this ascent is to carry within oneself the sworn promise to rescue, to intervene, to bring one's companions back to the surface.

At the end of each dive, like the hero of Homer finally sighting Ithaca on the horizon, they find the edge again. Faces flushed, eyes alight. The waters here need not envy the open seas. They too forge men and women worthy of an epic.

A note on the title: "L'eau d'ici par EauMer" plays in French on "Homer" — EauMer echoing the poet's name while literally meaning "water-sea". The English title preserves the signature and invites the attentive reader to hear, just beneath the surface, the name of the blind poet himself.
