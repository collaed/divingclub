📋 The "Boring (But Necessary)" Club Update

The "I Use Too Many Words" Edition
💶📊🚐🤿⚙️📋

Consider yourself warned: this is a boring update. I might not be the oldest member of the club, but I am certainly the most old-fashioned when it comes to communication—meaning, I use far too many words. Grab a coffee, and let's dive in.

We are a few months into the year, getting somewhat close to the end of the season already, with our first real trip coming up in just a few days. We knew taking this on would be a lot of work, and well... it is. And then some!

I cannot commend the past members of the bureau enough for keeping the boat afloat through all these years. They navigated some incredibly turbulent waters: times with no instructors left, a few internal crises, the total paralysis of Covid, and more recently, the dwindling flow of money.

Which brings me to some of our current realities.

The Financial Reality Check

The CAS sent us our funding. It's exactly €3,000. And they seem rather proud of themselves for it, which I can only interpret as a clear sign that le temps des vaches grasses (the fat cow years) is resolutely behind us.

Here is a quick breakdown of where we stand:

  • Members pay roughly €200 per year, but the club only keeps about €110 of that. The rest is immediately swallowed by federations and insurance.
  • Growing the club brings in a bit of extra budget, but the "little things" add up quickly.
  • Every brevet or qualification you receive costs us an extra €10 paid directly to the FFESSM.
  • Infrastructure and equipment maintenance is shockingly expensive. From spare parts and hours spent servicing gear to replacing compressor filters, it drains the accounts.
  • This year, we have to send about 40 tanks for TUV testing. We expect this to cost nearly €2,000 alone. Plus, we will inevitably lose a few tanks in the process (if they fail the certification renewal, they are destroyed).

The True Cost of a Dive

Even our routine trips to places like the Gravière du Fort (GdF) come with hidden price tags. We pay €5 per dive and €6 per tank filling. Over a standard weekend, a single diver's participation easily costs the club €38.

Pretty much all of us have experienced the Gravière. It's a fantastic, convenient place to reach 40m for certifications. But let's be honest—it isn't exactly the most exhilarating dive of your life, especially in the freezing chill of April or May. We are all deeply thankful to the instructors who take their time to go there and assist us. But I sometimes wonder what they actually get out of it, aside from the quiet satisfaction of a job well done. They even have to pay for their own accommodation just to freeze in a quarry with us! We are currently looking into ways to make these trips less financially taxing and more rewarding for them.

The bottom line is that money is scarce. It is this year, and it probably will be for the foreseeable future. We do not want to simply raise membership fees (though we might eventually have to), so we are hunting for ways to optimize our outcomes: making you spend less while costing the club less.

Logistics, Rules, and Cost-Cutting Ideas

  • Accommodation Hacks: When we go to the GdF for a weekend, we usually stay at the B&B Holtzheim. It's not luxury, but it is "functional." If we enroll in their loyalty program, we save €10 per night, per room. It's definitely worth it. However, the cardholder will have to handle bookings for everyone and will be personally liable for no-shows. If you cancel at the last minute, we cannot guarantee a refund. The benefits are there, but we will have to enforce strict rules, and on occasion, some people might not be happy about it. That's the price of a discount.
  • The End of Mileage Refunds: Reimbursing members' kilometers to drive to dives is simply no longer sustainable. Instead, by joining FLASSA, we gained access to cheap van rentals. We will organize transport for you. While charging you upfront for a seat in the van might feel like a downgrade from getting paid to drive, it actually prevents you from taking on the higher wear-and-tear costs of your own car (and it's greener). Because the club assumes the rental cost upfront, we will have to be strict about last-minute cancellations to protect the collective budget over individual interests.
  • Equipment Respect & Rentals: Our rules state that once certified, members should buy their own gear. We are pretty flexible about this, but please, respect the club's equipment. By now, our newer members have shopped around for their basic "PMT" (fins, mask, snorkel) and seen the prices. Renting gear independently is wildly expensive. We are exploring ways to save you money by offering cheaper rentals directly through the club or via negotiated partnerships.

The Administrative Future

Setting all this up will take time and investment. One of our biggest hurdles is that we do not legally exist; we are a collective, not a proper association. We are actively looking into the consequences of transitioning into an official ASBL. It will definitely add a layer of bureaucratic paperwork, but it could be the key to unlocking new revenue streams instead of just crossing our fingers and hoping for better luck with the CAS.

Other Miscellaneous Rumors

There are rumors that our access to the pools will become more complicated next season. They are just rumors for now, so there is nothing actionable yet, but we are ramping up our preparation just in case.

On a brighter note, starting next season, some of our "Fosses" will be organized by FLASSA. This might give us longer, later slots (like 20:00–21:30), meaning a more relaxed vibe and more time in the water. Though, whoever is stuck registering the wet gear returns at 22:00 might not find it so relaxing...
