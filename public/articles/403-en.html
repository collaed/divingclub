Trial by Fire (and Neoprene)

By Marta

It all starts with shoving all the gear (thanks Keran, what a mess!) into a bag that I could almost fit myself into without much trouble. While playing Tetris with bags of clothes, booties, fins, the regulator, the wetsuit, and all the other essentials for surviving underwater, I couldn't help but wonder how we were going to transport the gear of 7 people—with roughly the same amount of stuff—in a single vehicle.

(Very) early Sunday morning, my dad and I arrive at a parking lot where we're handed a minibus (I finally understand where all the bags are going), and the long journey to the coast begins. We arrive at the hotel, and immediately after dinner, we go to bed so we can wake up early the next day. I'm eager because this will be my first dive!

At some point, the alarm goes off. We have breakfast, get back in the minibus, and suddenly, I am reunited with my worst enemy... the wetsuit! It's hot, I'm sleepy, and I have to gather my things and pack them into a tiny room that smells worse and worse every day. I don't have a crate, so I have to make several back-and-forth trips to carry my stuff to the boat, all while trying to readjust this wetsuit.

Once on the boat, the battle with the wetsuit continues, but this time it's joined by the BCD, the tank, and 6 extra kilos of lead—as if all the rest wasn't enough.

Now we're moored to the buoy, and we're told what we're going to do, see, and the route we'll follow. In other words, Keran explains what he wants to do, and the rest of the club decides to follow him all week. On top of that, one of the days, I start using Nitrox and therefore have to do math—which is already a bad idea in itself—made even worse on a boat in the blazing sun, dealing with heat and fatigue... all while debating the name of a marker... which, of course, didn't work.

I repeat this twice a day for five days. Every day, I end up exhausted, with sore fingers and a desperate need to eat and shower. I even started thinking about suing my dad for moral and physical damages.

In short, I loved it. It was an incredible experience. I saw huge amounts of fish—colorful, shiny, beautiful, weird, up close, far away, in schools or solo... Corals with colors you can only see with a dive light, octopuses hiding in the rocks, lobsters, moray eels with unwelcoming faces but mesmerizing bodies...

If anyone asks if I recommend the experience, I will answer yes, without a doubt! Everything that might seem 'unpleasant' is nothing compared to the laughs, the scenery, the group atmosphere, the instructors, the lessons, and finally putting the pool theory into practice. I could go on, but I still wouldn't be able to explain just how awesome it was.

A huge thank you to Keran for lending all the necessary gear, to Eddy for being an excellent driver, to Pietro, Michel, and Jérôme for their eagerness to teach, to Fred for his bottomless pharmacy and constant care for everyone's well-being, and most especially to my dive buddy, Mafalda, for making this trip a total success.

A Father's Pride

By Marco, one of our senior members

Today, I was finally able to share my first dive with my daughter. After several months of training, she obtained her Level 1 certification without difficulty, and I couldn't be happier.

The weekend got off to the best possible start: no need to take the car. We travelled with other club members in a minibus, which made the journey far more convivial and relaxing.

On site, conditions were ideal. The rooms were simple but comfortable, everyone had their own space, and the atmosphere was very pleasant. The meals, home-cooked, were good and generous. There was even a small swimming pool where we could unwind between dives.

The dive site itself is not particularly renowned for its visibility or the abundance of marine life. But in the end, that matters little: being underwater is always an immense pleasure. For me, diving has always provided an incomparable sense of freedom and wellbeing. This time, that pleasure was even greater, as I was able to share it with my daughter. And the most wonderful part was seeing how much she enjoyed the experience.

We were also lucky to be joined by our friend Keran who, in addition to lending us some equipment, introduced us to the magnificent landscapes of the region.

All in all, it was an excellent trip. These club-organised outings have something special about them: you share great moments with people you know and are fond of. Personally, I find that more enjoyable than going off on your own, even with family.

I simply wanted to share this wonderful experience with you, and the joy I felt watching my daughter discover and fall in love with an activity that has held an important place in my life for over sixty years. I hope we will have many more opportunities to dive together in the years to come.

I wish you all wonderful dives and many moments of shared joy.
